# Indulgences for the Plano de Vida Practices

*A research report on the indulgences attached to each spiritual practice in the app.*

*It follows the Church's official* Enchiridion Indulgentiarum *(4th ed., 1999), read in the USCCB's English translation, the* Manual of Indulgences *(2006).*

*It also uses the Code of Canon Law, Paul VI's* Indulgentiarum Doctrina *(1967), the Apostolic Penitentiary's note* The Gift of the Indulgence *(2000), and opusdei.org's page on indulgences for members of the Work.*

*"Grant N" means a numbered grant ("concession") of the 1999 Manual; "Norm N" means one of its Norms on Indulgences.*

---

## 1. What an indulgence is

An **indulgence** remits the *temporal punishment* due to sins that have already been forgiven.

Two kinds:

- **Partial** — removes *part* of that punishment.

  No fixed "days" anymore (that system was abolished in 1967); the amount mirrors the fervor of the act.
- **Plenary** — removes *all* of it.

A partial indulgence is added *on top of* what the good work earns by itself: the faithful obtain, "in addition to the remission of temporal punishment acquired by the action itself, an equal remission of punishment through the intervention of the Church" (Norm 4).

---

## 2. A plenary indulgence is available every day

The Manual itself singles out four works by which "the faithful can obtain a plenary indulgence each day of the year" (Introduction to the grants, no. 7):

- **Adoration of the Blessed Sacrament for at least half an hour** (Grant 7 §1, 1°).
- **The Way of the Cross** (Grant 13, §2).
- **The Rosary** in a church or oratory, in a family, a religious community or an association of the faithful, or whenever several faithful gather for a good purpose (Grant 17 §1, 1°).
- **Reading Sacred Scripture for at least half an hour** (Grant 30).

The limit is **one plenary a day** (Norm 18 §1), so in practice one plenary every day is within reach, given the conditions in §9.

## 3. Indulgences per practice

| Practice (PT) | Indulgence | Grant | Conditions and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Santo Rosário** | **Plenary** | 17 §1 | Five decades **without interruption**, **vocal prayer + meditation on the mysteries**, said **in a church/oratory, in a family, a religious community, an association of the faithful, or whenever several faithful gather**. Also plenary: joining the Pope's rosary broadcast live on radio or TV. **Partial** in other circumstances, e.g. said alone. If a reasonable cause stops you mid-way, the decades said earn a partial (Introduction, no. 6). |
| **Ladainha** (Litany of Loreto, in the Rosary) | Partial | 22, 2° | Reciting an approved litany. |
| **Visita ao Santíssimo** | **Plenary** | 7 §1, 1° | Adoration of **at least half an hour**. **Partial** for a shorter visit (7 §2, 1°). |
| **Adoro te devote** (Thursdays) | Partial | 7 §2, 2° | Named in the grant as an example of a prayer to Jesus in the Blessed Sacrament. |
| **Leitura do Novo Testamento** (Sacred Scripture) | **Plenary** | 30 | Read as spiritual reading, from an approved text, with reverence, for **at least half an hour**. **Partial** if shorter, so the app's default 5-minute reading is partial. If you cannot read for a good reason, listening to someone reading, or to a recording, counts the same (30 §2). |
| **Leitura Espiritual** (other books) | — | — | Only *Scripture* reading is indulgenced as such; spiritual reading of other books has no specific grant. |
| **Oração mental** | Partial | 15 | "To the faithful who for their personal edification devoutly spend time in mental prayer." |
| **Ângelus** / **Regina Coeli** | Partial | 17 §2, 2° | At dawn, noon or evening, with its versicles and prayer; the Regina Coeli with its prayer in Eastertide. |
| **Lembrai-vos**, **Salve Rainha** (Saturday antiphon), *Sub tuum* | Partial | 17 §2, 3° | Addressing Our Lady with an approved prayer; the Memorare, Salve Regina and Sub tuum are the grant's own examples. |
| **Comunhão Espiritual** | Partial | 8 §2, 1° | An act of spiritual communion in any approved formula. |
| **Ação de graças após a Comunhão** (*Anima Christi*) | Partial | 8 §2, 2° | **Plenary** on the **Fridays of Lent** for praying *En ego, O bone et dulcissime Iesu* before a crucifix after Communion (8 §1, 2°). |
| **Exame de Consciência** | Partial | 9 | "Examine their conscience with the purpose of amendment", especially in preparation for confession (9, 1°). Reciting an act of contrition, e.g. the Confiteor, is a further partial (9, 2°). |
| **Via Sacra** | **Plenary** | 13 §2 | Made **before legitimately erected stations**, moving from station to station; meditating on the Passion is enough, without reflecting on each station. Those legitimately impeded gain it by reading and meditating on the Passion for about a quarter of an hour. |
| **Recolhimento mensal** | Partial | 10 §2 | Taking part in a monthly recollection. A **retreat of at least three full days** gives a **plenary** (10 §1). |
| **Oferecimento de Obras** / **Oferecimento do Trabalho** | Partial | 26 §2; General Grant 1 | A prayer at the beginning of the day or when starting work (26 §2). It is also the classic case of raising the mind to God in one's duties (General Grant 1). opusdei.org lists "offering one's works to God" among the partials. |
| **Preces** | Partial, for many of its prayers | see §4 | The Preces as a whole is not a grant, but many of its prayers are grants on their own. |
| **Te Deum** (31 Dec) / **Veni Creator** (1 Jan, Pentecost) | **Plenary** | 26 §1 | For **public** recitation or singing on those days. Said privately, or on other days, both are partial (26 §2). |
| **Novenas** | Partial (conditional) | 22, 1° | The grant covers **public novenas**, e.g. before Christmas, Pentecost or the Immaculate Conception. A private novena to St. Josemaría has no specific grant, though its prayers can earn partials (§8, General Grant 1). |
| **Oração a São Josemaria** | Partial (general) | General Grant 1 | No specific concession in the Manual; earns a partial as a pious invocation. |
| **Três Ave-Marias** | Probably partial | (17 §2, 3°) | Indulgenced in the **old Raccolta**, but not named in the 1999 Manual. As approved prayers to Our Lady they plausibly fall under 17 §2, 3° — that is an inference, not a named grant. |
| **Santa Missa** | — / Plenary (special cases) | 8 §1; 27 | Attending Mass is not itself listed as an indulgenced work, and a work already imposed by law, such as Sunday Mass, does not gain an indulgence unless the grant says so (Norm 21 §1). Communion received at Mass fulfills a plenary condition. **Plenary** on special occasions: your First Communion or assisting at someone else's (8 §1, 1°); a priest's first Mass, and the 25th, 50th and 60th anniversaries of ordination (27). |
| **Beijar a cruz de madeira** (Opus Dei oratories) | Partial | particular grant | Kissing the wooden cross while saying a short prayer; listed by opusdei.org. |

### Bottom line for the plan of life

- **Can yield a PLENARY indulgence** (with the conditions in §9): Rosário (in family or community), Visita ao Santíssimo (≥30 min), Leitura do Novo Testamento (≥30 min), Via Sacra (in a church with stations).
- **Yield a PARTIAL indulgence:** Oração mental, Ângelus, Lembrai-vos, Salve Rainha, Adoro te, Comunhão Espiritual, Exame de Consciência, Recolhimento mensal, the offerings, many prayers of the Preces, the Rosário said alone, shorter Scripture reading, the Ladainha, the prayer to St. Josemaría, and very probably the Três Ave-Marias.
- **No specific grant:** Leitura Espiritual of books other than Scripture, and attending Mass as such.

## 4. The Preces

opusdei.org: "It is also possible to obtain a partial indulgence by reciting a short prayer while kissing the wooden cross in Opus Dei oratories, offering one's works to God, visiting the Blessed Sacrament, saying the Angelus or Regina Coeli, reciting a Marian antiphon on Saturdays, saying the Adoro te devote, making a spiritual communion, praying many of the prayers that form part of the Preces of the Work, and so on."

The Preces text bundled in the app, compared with the Manual:

| Prayer in the Preces | Grant | Indulgence |
|---|---|---|
| *Orémus pro Beatíssimo Papa nostro N.* | 25, 1° — the *Oremus pro Pontifice* is the grant's own example | Partial |
| *Retribúere dignáre, Dómine…* (for benefactors) | 24 — the same prayer is the grant's example | Partial |
| *Sancti Ángeli Custódes nostri…* | 18 — invoking one's guardian angel with an approved prayer | Partial |
| *Fecit te Deus quasi Patrem Regis…* (St. Joseph) | 19 — invoking St. Joseph with an approved prayer | Partial |
| *Recordáre, Virgo Mater Dei…* | 17 §2, 3° — addressing Our Lady with an approved prayer | Partial |
| *Réquiem ætérnam…* | 29 §2, 2° | Partial, **applicable only to the dead** |
| *Actiónes nostras…* (in the closing collect) | 26 §2 — named as an example | Partial, but only at the beginning or end of the day or of work |

The St. Joseph and Our Lady rows are a judgment that these verses count as approved prayers in the sense of the grant; the other rows match the Manual by name.

So the Preces, prayed with a general intention (§10), plausibly gain several partial indulgences at once.

## 5. Opus Dei's particular indulgences

Granted by the Holy See to the Prelature (opusdei.org; Apostolic Penitentiary decree Prot.

No. 1118/22/I of 22 December 2022, updating Prot.

No. 682/07/I of 2008; rescript 34/14 of 2014).

**Plenary**, for the faithful of the Work who devoutly renew their commitments with Opus Dei, and for cooperators who renew their decision to be cooperators:

- 14 February — beginning of the apostolate with women and of the Priestly Society of the Holy Cross.
- 19 March — St. Joseph.
- 29 June — Sts.

  Peter and Paul.
- 14 September — Exaltation of the Holy Cross.
- 29 September — Sts.

  Michael, Gabriel and Raphael.
- 2 October — foundation of Opus Dei and the Holy Guardian Angels.
- 27 December — St. John the Apostle.

Also **plenary**:

- For the faithful of the Work, on the day of their admission, incorporation and fidelity, and on the 25th, 50th, 60th and 75th anniversaries of admission.
- For cooperators, on the day of their enrollment.
- On the feast of the Holy Family, for the parents, brothers and sisters of faithful of the Work, and for the spouses and children of supernumeraries, who take part in a sacred function.
- In the triduum before Christmas and Easter, for anyone taking part in the liturgical celebrations the Prelature organizes then.

The usual conditions (§9) apply to all of these.

## 6. For the faithful departed (Grant 29)

**Plenary, applicable only to the souls in purgatory:**

- On **each day from 1 to 8 November**, for devoutly visiting a cemetery and praying for the dead, even only mentally.
- Each day is a separate opportunity, not a series to complete: one visit gains one plenary, and visiting on several days gains one each day (one Communion and one prayer for the Pope per plenary, §9).
- On **All Souls' Day** (2 November; or, if the Ordinary so decides, the Sunday before or after, or All Saints), for devoutly visiting a church or oratory and reciting an Our Father and the Creed.

**Partial, applicable only to the souls in purgatory:**

- Visiting a cemetery and praying for the dead, even mentally, on any day of the year.
- Reciting lauds or vespers of the Office of the Dead, or the *Requiem aeternam*.

## 7. Family

- **Act of Family Consecration** (Grant 1): a **plenary** for the members of the family on the day the family is first consecrated to the Sacred Heart or to the Holy Family, if possible by a priest or deacon, reciting the approved prayer before an image of the Sacred Heart or of the Holy Family.

  **Partial** on each anniversary.
- The **Rosary in family** is one of the four daily plenaries (§2).
- No grant in the 1999 Manual mentions spouses, weddings or rings (see §12).

## 8. The four "general grants" (each a PARTIAL indulgence)

These sweep up many of the items above.

A partial indulgence is granted to the faithful who:

1. **Raise their minds in humble trust to God** while carrying out their duties and bearing life's hardships, adding (at least mentally) a pious invocation. → *covers morning offering, offering of work, ejaculatory prayers, prayer to St. Josemaría*
2. **Give of themselves or their goods** in a spirit of faith and mercy to serve those in need.
3. **Voluntarily abstain, in a spirit of penance**, from something licit and pleasing to them.
4. **Openly bear witness to their faith** before others in the circumstances of daily life.

Jaculatórias belong to the first grant.

The Manual notes that an invocation "is not to be considered a complete or distinct work in itself, but rather is to be used as a complement" to raising the mind to God during one's duties, and that it "can be very short… or even merely conceived in the mind" (appendix *Pious Invocations*).

## 9. Conditions to actually earn an indulgence

### Prerequisites for *any* indulgence (plenary or partial)

You must be (Norm 17):

- **Baptized** and **not excommunicated**
- **In the state of grace** — at least by the time you finish the indulgenced work
- Acting with **at least a general intention** to gain the indulgence (see §10)
- Performing the prescribed work **at the time and in the manner required**

Unless the grant says otherwise, a work already imposed by law or precept does not gain an indulgence (Norm 21 §1).

### Partial indulgence — that's essentially it

Only the prerequisites above, plus doing the indulgenced act with at least a contrite heart (Norm 4).

**No** confession, Communion, or papal prayer required.

You can gain **multiple partial indulgences in a single day** (Norm 18 §1).

The Church supplements the remission in proportion to the charity and contrition you bring.

### Plenary indulgence — the prerequisites *plus* four more things (Norm 20)

1. **The indulgenced work itself** (e.g. Rosary in family, ½-hour adoration, ½-hour Scripture reading).
2. **Sacramental Confession**
3. **Eucharistic Communion**
4. **Prayer for the intentions of the Supreme Pontiff** — "fully satisfied by reciting one Our Father and one Hail Mary"; any other prayer for this intention also counts (Norm 20 §5).

   This means the Pope's general intentions for the Church, **not** his private intentions.
5. **Complete detachment from all sin, even venial.**

   The hardest condition.

   If any attachment to sin remains — or any of the above is missing — the indulgence is **reduced to partial** (Norm 20 §4).

### Timing & frequency rules for plenary

- **Same-day not strictly required:** Confession, Communion, and the papal prayer may be done "several days before or after" the work (Norm 20 §3).

  The Apostolic Penitentiary puts this at "several days (about 20) before or after the indulgenced act" (*The Gift of the Indulgence*, no. 5).

  It is *fitting* that Communion and the papal prayer happen on the day of the work.
- **One Confession → many plenaries:** a single sacramental confession can serve for **several** plenary indulgences (Norm 20 §2).
- **One Communion + one papal prayer → one plenary:** these two must be **repeated separately for each** plenary indulgence (Norm 20 §2).
- **One plenary per day maximum** (Norm 18 §1) — exception: at the hour of death one can be gained even if one was already gained that day (Norm 18 §2; Grant 12).

### Bonus rule

If an indulgenced work is **also** assigned as a sacramental penance in Confession, the **same act** both satisfies the penance *and* earns the indulgence (Norm 21 §2).

## 10. The intention: when and how

Norm 17 §2: "To gain an indulgence, one must have at least the general intention of doing so."

Canon 996 §2 says the same.

- **You don't have to name each indulgence**, or form the intention at the moment of the work.

  A general intention covers every indulgenced work of the day.
- **Traditional practice: once, in the morning.**

  The 1910 Raccolta (decisions of the Sacred Congregation of Indulgences): "A general intention of gaining all Indulgences is sufficient. It should be renewed from time to time, say every morning."
- The *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1910) requires "the intention, at least habitual, of gaining the indulgence".
- In the plan of life, the natural moment is the **Oferecimento de Obras**, with a sentence such as: "I intend to gain all the indulgences I can today, and I apply them to the souls in purgatory."

### Applying an indulgence

- Any indulgence, partial or plenary, can be applied **to oneself** or **to the dead** by way of suffrage, never to another living person (Norm 3; *The Gift of the Indulgence*, no. 7; can.

  994).
- The application can also be made by a general intention.

  The 1910 Raccolta: "By a further general intention all, or by a particular intention any, Indulgences may be applied to the Holy Souls in Purgatory, either to the Holy Souls in general, or to individual souls."
- For the dead it is a suffrage: the Church petitions God to accept the satisfaction for them, so it is not a guaranteed transfer the way self-application is (*Catholic Encyclopedia*).
- You don't need to name a specific soul — you can offer it generally "for the souls in purgatory".
- The one-plenary-per-day limit applies regardless of who you offer it for.
- The original version of this report also said that each indulgence goes to either yourself or one deceased soul and cannot be split between both.

  That has not been re-verified against a primary source.

## 11. What happens if you forget

- **No intention at all**, not even a standing one from the morning offering: the indulgence is not gained.

  The work keeps its own value — Norm 4 describes the Church's remission as given "in addition to" what the act earns by itself — so forgetting loses the Church's added remission, not the good of the prayer.
- **No application chosen:** the common teaching is that the indulgence then benefits the person who gained it.

  No primary text found for this; unverified.
- **A plenary condition missed** (e.g. no Communion within the window, or attachment to sin remains): the indulgence becomes **partial**, it is not lost (Norm 20 §4).

## 12. Claims checked

### "Kissing your spouse's wedding ring gives an indulgence"

**Not a current grant.**

The 1999 Manual has no grant mentioning spouses, weddings or rings.

The likely origin is the 1910 Raccolta, no. 491: "Kissing the Ring of a Cardinal or Bishop. 50 Days" (Pius X, 18 March 1909).

That is a bishop's ring, not a spouse's, and no spouse-ring entry was found in that edition either.

The 1967 reform abolished the "days" system and ordered the collection revised "with a view to attaching indulgences only to the most important prayers and works of piety, charity and penance" (*Indulgentiarum Doctrina*, n. 13).

Kissing the ring while raising the mind to God with a short invocation would plausibly fall under the first general grant (§8), as any ordinary act done that way.

That is an inference from the grant's wording, not a grant about rings.

## 13. Practical takeaway

- **Each morning**, in the Oferecimento de Obras, make the general intention to gain every indulgence of the day, and say where you apply them.
- The **partial-indulgence** practices (Oração mental, Ângelus, Lembrai-vos, Comunhão Espiritual, Exame, offerings, the Preces, etc.) just need you in a state of grace with that intention — do them freely all day; they stack.
- For a **plenary** (Rosary in family, ½-hour Visita, ½-hour Scripture, Via Sacra in a church), line up regular Confession + Communion + the papal prayer (an Our Father and a Hail Mary) — and, the real work, strive for detachment from all sin.
- You can secure **one plenary per day**; everything else accumulates as partials, applicable to yourself or to the souls in purgatory.
- Mark the **Opus Dei dates** (§5) and **1–8 November** (§6) in the calendar.
